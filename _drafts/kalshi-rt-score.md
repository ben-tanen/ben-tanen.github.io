Throughout 2025, before I largely stopped using TikTok, I was being inundated with ads from Kalshi, the prediction market / gamble-on-whatever-you-want app that you are probably already well aware of. It got to the point that it felt like literally every ad I got on TikTok was from Kalshi exclusively. One of the more common ads I was getting was this one where they discuss betting on whether *M3GAN 2.0* would have a Rotten Tomatoes score over/under 75%.

{% include video.html src="/assets/video/posts/kalshi-rt-score-45920cdf.mp4" %}

I think I started getting this ad after [I reluctantly watched M3GAN 2.0 on an airplane](https://letterboxd.com/btanen/film/m3gan-20/) (months after the film actually released in June 2025), which always left me confused as to why Kalshi was paying for such outdated ads. But more than just confused on how Kalshi is spending [their millions of dollars](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/kalshi-fee-revenue-2025-263-145801350.html), I was sort of just grossed out at the idea of betting on the already somewhat reductive numerical measure of Rotten Tomatoes score for what is supposed to be an at-least-somewhat artistic effort – I recognize defending the artistic nature of *M3GAN 2.0* is a bit of a stretch, but I’m here doing it anyway! So, while I already didn’t like the pervasiveness of sports gambling and prediction markets as is, I thought: how about we just don’t do this version of it too?

I eventually stopped using TikTok but months later, I still think about this ad all the time and the other day it dawned on me: making bets on the synthetic numerical score of a piece of art is 2026’s version of the synthetic CDO from 2008, as nicely explained by the Big Short. It’s all just derivatives of derivatives!

{% include youtube.html id="A25EUhZGBws" start="201" %}

The Big Short was all about obvious indicators of good things to come in the future, right…?

Now I know I was only 12 when the 2008 Financial Crisis hit, but I don’t really remember there being that many ads for buying synthetic CDOs on Nickelodeon (the TikTok of 2008). But it’s probably okay that today we’re marketing derivative-of-derivative-of-derivative gambling on prediction markets to kids via TikTok.
